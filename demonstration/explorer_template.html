1. Start with the data

Each raw recording has seven columns: time in seconds, acceleration X/Y/Z in g, and angular velocity X/Y/Z in rad/s. Diagnoses come from the explicit condition column; numeric label 2 combines several differential diagnoses and is not an ET-only label.

The cohort comparison below uses 304 independent participants. Healthy participants provide an illustrative reference in the explorer, but are not part of the PD-versus-ET cohort comparison.

2. Explore a recording and its Fourier spectrum

Examples are the smallest published participant ID in each condition: PD 004, ET 066, and healthy 001. Selection did not use spectral strength or apparent separation between diagnoses. All 11 tasks and both wrists are available for these three people.

The 3–12 Hz region is an exploratory analysis band. The displayed peak is the largest Welch PSD bin in that band; it is calculated even when no clinically meaningful tremor is present. It does not assign a diagnosis.

Time traces show the interpolated, linearly detrended axes after omitting the first 0.5 seconds. Spectra use all retained samples; the chart displays 0–20 Hz from the full 0–50 Hz spectrum. Drag to zoom and double-click to reset. Click legend items to isolate axes. Band metrics always use the Welch sum of three axis PSDs, independent of the selected display method.

3. Follow the Fourier calculation

  1. Check the recording checksum, shape, finite values, and strictly increasing timestamps.
  2. Interpolate each axis onto a uniform 100 Hz grid inside the original time support; omit the initial 0.5 s notification interval.
  3. Subtract each axis's least-squares linear trend. This removes a fitted baseline, not all effects of changing sensor orientation.
  4. Apply a periodic Hann window before the real FFT. Correct the one-sided amplitude by the window's coherent gain.
  5. For PSD, divide squared FFT magnitude by fs × sum(window²). Double interior positive-frequency bins, leaving DC and an even-length Nyquist bin unchanged.
  6. For Welch PSD, average 4 s periodic-Hann segment periodograms with 50% overlap and a mean subtraction within each segment.

4. Compare independent participants

For Relaxed and StretchHold, calculate each wrist's sum of three axis Welch PSDs, then average the two wrist PSDs. Every participant contributes once per task and sensor. Curves show group medians; shading shows the interquartile range across participants.

These are descriptive cohort summaries. They do not estimate classification performance or establish that Fourier peak frequency separates PD from ET. The cohorts are imbalanced (276 versus 28), their distributions overlap, and movement-task/device/treatment context can affect spectra.

6. Reproduce and interpret

The executed notebook, source code, exact dependencies, per-recording quality checks, and participant-level feature tables accompany this report. The notebook explains the DFT and one-sided scaling algebra and lets you change participant, task, wrist, and sensor.

Timestamp quality:

The sensitivity view retains a participant for a task only when the largest timestamp gap in each entire raw wrist recording is at most 30 ms (three nominal intervals). This is an exploratory quality threshold, not a clinically validated cutoff. The selected cohort composition changes, and the retained counts are displayed for each group.

Linear interpolation addresses recorded timing jitter and cannot reconstruct motion lost during sampling gaps. Wrist averaging can dilute one-sided symptoms. Axis-summed PSD avoids taking the Fourier transform of the nonlinear vector magnitude, which can alter frequency content.

All published PD/ET assignments are retained, including eight PD-assigned participants whose comments mention essential tremor. No diagnosis changes, model fitting, or train/test claims are part of this demonstration. Any later classifier evaluation should split by participant before making signal windows.
